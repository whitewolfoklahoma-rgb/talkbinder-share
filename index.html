Every certification is entered once, on the worker's record. The matrix below is figured from those records — it is never a second spreadsheet somebody has to keep in step.

CertificationRequired forCurrentExpiring ≤30 daysExpiredMissing
How expiry is figured: a certification counts as expired the day after its expiry date, and "expiring" within 30 days of it. Certifications with no expiry date (like the OSHA 10-hour card) count as current. For forklift operators, TalkBinder tracks the operator evaluation: OSHA's powered-industrial-truck rule requires each operator to be evaluated at least once every three years (29 CFR 1910.178(l)(4)(iii)) — the evaluation due date is what belongs in the expiry field. VERIFY: your own company policy and your GC's requirements set which certifications each role must hold and how often they renew — the required-for lists here are your company's settings, not OSHA's.

Incidents & the OSHA 300 log

Every incident is reported here once. The ones that meet OSHA's recording criteria appear on the 300 log below — figured from the same record, never re-typed. TalkBinder prepares the log; the owner makes the final call on every case.

Recording criteria — 29 CFR 1904.7: a work-related injury or illness goes on the 300 log if it results in any of: death · days away from work · restricted work or transfer to another job · medical treatment beyond first aid · loss of consciousness · a significant injury or illness diagnosed by a physician or other licensed health care professional. First aid is the closed list at 1904.7(b)(5) — for example non-prescription medication at non-prescription strength, tetanus shots, hot/cold therapy, and bandages. Anything not on that list is medical treatment. Days away are counted in calendar days starting the day after the injury, capped at 180 (1904.7(b)(3)–(4)). Each case is entered on the log within 7 calendar days (1904.29(b)(3)). Separately, OSHA's reporting rule sets short clocks for telling OSHA about severe events — a fatality within 8 hours; an inpatient hospitalization, an amputation, or the loss of an eye within 24 hours (1904.39). This helper is guidance drawn from the regulation text, not a ruling — borderline cases are the owner's call, and your safety consultant or OSHA's recordkeeping page is the authority. Some very small employers are partially exempt from keeping the log unless asked (1904.1) — VERIFY your own status.

Under 29 CFR 1904.32, at year end the company reviews the log, totals it on the 300A summary, has a company executive certify it, and posts it where employee notices go from February 1 through April 30. The log, the summary, and the incident reports are kept for five years (1904.33). TalkBinder figures the totals; the executive's certification and the posting are the company's to do.

Help

Short answers for the jobs TalkBinder does. Everything runs on this device; records are stored in this browser only.

How do I run a toolbox talk?

Open Toolbox Talks, tap a topic from the library (or type your own), fill in the date, project, and who led it, tick who attended, and save. The sign-off sheet opens on the same device — pass it around, each attendee signs with a finger, and the signatures are filed with the talk.

How does the training matrix stay current?

It is figured from the certification records every time it is shown. Add or renew a certification once — on the Training & Certs screen or from the worker's wallet — and the matrix, the chase list, and the dashboard all move together.

What counts for the OSHA 300 log?

The criteria in 29 CFR 1904.7, listed on the OSHA 300 screen: death, days away, restricted work or transfer, medical treatment beyond first aid, loss of consciousness, or a significant injury or illness diagnosed by a physician or other licensed health care professional. TalkBinder shows guidance from the regulation text; the final decision on every case belongs to the owner.

What does TalkBinder not do?

TalkBinder prepares paperwork. It does not decide whether an injury is recordable, does not certify that your company is compliant, does not file anything with OSHA or anyone else, and does not replace your safety program, your competent persons, or your own judgment. Certifications and evaluations are performed by qualified people in the real world — TalkBinder keeps the records of them.

Where does my data live, and how do I back it up?

On this device, in this browser's storage — nothing is uploaded. Back up from Settings below (Export backup saves a file of every record); import that file on any device to move or restore the binder. Export before you replace a phone or clear browser data.
